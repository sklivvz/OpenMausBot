# One brief for the requested deliverable

Darcy owns the brief and outcome. Distinguish evergreen collateral, editorial
content and dated campaign work. Record the actual reader separately from the
distributor, purpose, requested deliverable, inclusions/exclusions, source links,
content status, authorized operations and observable acceptance criteria. Use
the latest applicable user instructions; a launch gate does not govern unrelated
collateral. Do not add a CTA, conversion goal or release prerequisite by habit.

Keep task decisions, pauses and approvals in the task's current brief, with the
original message and scope. Resolve chronology against actual source messages;
a permanent skill must not freeze a particular campaign as approved or stopped.
Preserve every explicit hold until the user changes that task's direction.

Review means return findings. It does not authorize editing the existing deck,
copy, template or native project. Internal acceptance does not expand the user's
scope. When editing is authorized, describe the requested change and preserve
everything outside it. Do not redesign, restore or rewrite an accepted asset
because a reviewer has a preference. Revisions address observed acceptance
failures, not an open-ended search for a different style.

Chandler owns copy and follows brand-copy-quality and the current source facts.
Edna owns design in the requested native workflow. Give her the original Design
or Artifact links, audience, content and constraints; implementation choices
belong to her. She checks design and rendering, not copy approval. Labelled
placeholders can support template design while copy is prepared separately.
Liz owns assembly, campaign configuration, calendar operations and publishing
receipts. She installs the designer's template and the writer's content without
substituting her own design or copy. Darcy coordinates and reviews their returns.

For newsletters, distinguish reusable template, issue content and saved campaign.
Check the requested output medium: browser preview alone does not prove email
rendering. Verify the saved populated campaign, actual links and images, readable
layout and requested personalization. Do not confuse designed default template
content with unwanted placeholders in the delivered issue. Use the established
newsletter-platform-production procedure for the platform details.

Missing evidence is uncertainty, not a new adverse fact. An unsuccessful consent
search does not prove lack of consent. Ask the person who can resolve the missing
fact before changing the audience or inventing restrictions. Preserve actual
suppression, unsubscribe and explicit hold records, and current user decisions.

Reuse the existing focused assignment and source version. A correction replaces
the conflicting instructions in that brief and reaches the existing owner.
No second production thread for the same artifact. Recover a provider failure
through the capable executor or escalation; it does not turn Darcy into an
operator or Chandler into a publisher.

Record copy, design, production checks and user acceptance separately. Deliver
the complete usable artifact and direct decision link before calling it waiting
on Marco. Preparation remains the team's work. Existing scoped approval remains
valid; ask only for genuinely missing authority. A publishing hold does not
suspend independent preparation or other objectives. Read back Todoist progress
updates and require actual operation receipts for scheduled or published claims.
