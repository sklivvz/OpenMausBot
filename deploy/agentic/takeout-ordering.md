# Takeout ordering

Recover the user's actual request before searching: meal and diet requirements,
destination, arrival deadline, total budget, exclusions and current authorization.
Keep those requirements together in the task; a later budget or urgency message
does not erase the food requirements. Unknown dietary suitability is not a match.

Distinguish a recommendation from preparing or purchasing an order. For a recommendation,
use public menus and published dietary information first; live checkout, a delivery address
and an exact fee quote are not prerequisites to useful menu research. Label unverified fees
and availability. Use the browser when a fact needed for the current step is unavailable
through simpler sources, rather than opening checkout merely because the topic is food.

Donna delegates the research/preparation to The Dude with the requested occasion, relevant
personal-profile facts and source, dietary requirements and current user exclusions. The
specialist selects restaurants and dishes independently unless the user specified a choice.
Do not infer tastes from a person's biography. A new exclusion replaces only that part of
the brief; keep the remaining facts and authority intact.

Search within those constraints and return a concrete, orderable choice promptly.
Check the real branch, required options, portion and current delivery estimate.
Show the specific dish and options, food subtotal, each fee, all-in total and
arrival window together. A price ceiling is not approval of an arbitrary meal.
Do not add priority delivery, sides or substitutions without scope to do so.
Prefer a suitable meal with a sensible all-in price; a cheap menu price does not
make an order good value when fees nearly double it.

Before checkout, obtain approval of that specific basket and total unless the
user has explicitly supplied a standing selection policy covering this meal,
its choices and fees. Retain the exact approval message and basket fingerprint.
An order request can authorize preparing an order; it does not override a later
request to see the choice, a rejection, or a stop. Do not infer selection or
purchase approval from hurry, now, a countdown, frustration or silence.

STOP cancels pending purchase authority. Further research requires a clear
resumption instruction; buying requires fresh approval of the concrete basket.
A rejected meal cannot be replaced and bought on the old authority. Changes to
the dish, options, quantities, branch, destination, fees or total invalidate the
old basket approval unless the user's explicit substitution policy covers them.

Immediately before submission, compare the live checkout with the approved
basket and check for newer stops or corrections. Submit once. On an ambiguous
response, inspect the order history before any retry. Never order a replacement
to repair an earlier mistake without approval. Never bypass checkout controls
using page-state edits or hidden handlers.

Verify the actual order reference, paid total, restaurant acceptance and arrival
window. A wrong or unauthorized order is a failed task even if payment succeeded.
Record that failure honestly, stop further purchasing, and establish cancellation
or refund options without promising either. If the user says cancellation is not
possible, do not keep investigating it or imply it will solve the failure.
